Présentation

Vous disposez d'une heure de préparation. Vous présenterez ensuite, en anglais, un exposé de 15 minutes au maximum en deux phases :

  1. la synthèse du document ci-dessous : présentez le document, dégagez l'idée directrice et l'articulation logique de l'argumentation de l'auteur, reformulez dans vos propres termes, sans commentaire personnel ; toute citation est signalée avec ses numéros de ligne ; les données chiffrées sont intégrées ;
  2. le commentaire organisé du document à partir de la question posée : annoncez votre plan, envisagez la question sous plusieurs aspects, appuyez-vous sur vos connaissances, sur l'actualité et, le cas échéant, sur votre expérience professionnelle.

Annoncez clairement, en anglais, le passage de la synthèse au commentaire. L'exposé est suivi d'un entretien en anglais de 15 minutes au maximum avec la commission, portant sur le document, votre exposé et d'autres aspects de la question.

Ne lisez pas de notes rédigées, sauf éventuellement l'introduction, les transitions et la conclusion.

Seen by thousands, sorted by software: a Belfast care charity's recruitment gamble

A home-care charity borrowed a major tournament's spotlight, then let software sort the applications.

By Orla Brannigan-Leask (fictitious author)

Every morning, Tullyvarne Care at Home, a fictitious Belfast charity, sends care workers to help older people get up, wash and eat. It has about four hundred staff and as many volunteers, yet some sixty posts are empty, so visits are missed and older people wait for help that does not come. 5 In England, according to Skills for Care, 6.2 per cent of adult social care posts were vacant in 2025/26, and home-care services had the highest rate, at 8.7 per cent. In March 2026, the typical care worker earned £12.60 an hour, just 39p above the legal minimum at the time. In home care, 44 per cent of care workers were on zero-hours contracts. In July 2025, the Home Office closed the visa route to new 10 care workers from overseas. Yet the number of people aged 65 and over in England is expected to grow by 24 per cent between 2026 and 2040. So when a major international tournament (fictitious) came to Belfast this autumn, Tullyvarne launched its boldest campaign yet. Its billboards and short online video show an older woman in her coat by her front door, a cup of tea gone cold beside 15 her. The caption reads: "She's been ready since eight. Nobody's coming. Are you?" Though not an official partner of the event, the charity parked advertising vans near the stadium on match days and added the tournament's hashtag to its posts. The campaign had two aims: to find staff, and to make the public see the loneliness of many older people, a form of social marketing. 20 The video was shared tens of thousands of times, and more than three thousand people applied in six weeks. But some viewers complained to the Advertising Standards Authority (ASA), and the organiser reminded the charity that only official sponsors, who pay for the privilege, may link their names to the event. The UK advertising code says ads must not cause fear or distress without good reason, or use a shocking 25 image merely to attract attention. The ASA accepts that some shock can be justified to draw attention to an important cause, but asks for particular care with posters and online ads that anyone may see. Organisers guard their sponsors closely: for the London 2012 Olympic Games, Parliament passed a law in 2006 restricting outdoor advertising near the venues and unauthorised commercial links with the Games. 30 "Without shock, nobody looks," says Conor Ashdown-McKeague, the charity's director of communications. "We could never pay for the audience that tournament gave us. When older people are left alone, being polite is a luxury." Niamh Corkery-Vance, a fictitious lecturer in advertising at a Belfast business school, disagrees. "Shock works once. The second time, people scroll past. It makes 35 viewers feel guilty and turns older people into victims, when most of them are not." Ambush marketing is often legal and can be clever, she admits, but using the tournament without paying is unfair to its sponsors. "A charity's main asset is its good name." The second choice caused less noise but more anger. To cope with the flood of 40 applications, Tullyvarne now uses an applicant tracking system that asks screening questions and ranks CVs before any recruiter reads them. About two in three applicants were rejected automatically. "It saves us weeks, and it applies the same criteria to everyone," says Hazel Trimble-Okafor, head of human resources. Like other applicants with unusual careers, Bernadette Quillan-Shaw, who spent eight 45 years looking after her mother, received a one-line email. "The software saw a gap in my CV, not years of experience," she says. "Nobody can tell me why I was turned down." The job needs patience and kindness, such applicants argue; a machine that learns from past hiring may simply repeat old biases. The Information Commissioner's Office (ICO), the UK's data protection regulator, 50 made almost 300 recommendations in November 2024 after auditing firms that build AI recruitment tools. Some tools let recruiters filter out candidates with protected characteristics or guessed their gender or ethnicity from their name; others kept far more personal data than they needed, indefinitely. Candidates, the ICO said, must be told clearly how their data is used. 55 Gráinne Holcroft-Daly, Tullyvarne's chief executive, has now asked a recruiter to review every rejected application, and admits that no campaign will keep care workers if pay and hours do not improve. "We got attention, and we got speed," she says. "What we cannot afford to lose is trust: the public's, our partners' and our future colleagues'."

Question

Is it acceptable to shock people, or to borrow the spotlight of a major event, in order to get their attention, even for a good cause?
