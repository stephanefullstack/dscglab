## Présentation

Vous disposez d'une heure de préparation. Vous présenterez ensuite, en anglais, un exposé de 15 minutes au maximum en deux phases :

1. la **synthèse** du document ci-dessous : présentez le document, dégagez l'idée directrice et l'articulation logique de l'argumentation de l'auteur, reformulez dans vos propres termes, sans commentaire personnel ; toute citation est signalée avec ses numéros de ligne ; les données chiffrées sont intégrées ;
2. le **commentaire organisé** du document à partir de la question posée : annoncez votre plan, envisagez la question sous plusieurs aspects, appuyez-vous sur vos connaissances, sur l'actualité et, le cas échéant, sur votre expérience professionnelle.

Annoncez clairement, en anglais, le passage de la synthèse au commentaire. L'exposé est suivi d'un **entretien en anglais** de 15 minutes au maximum avec la commission, portant sur le document, votre exposé et d'autres aspects de la question.

Ne lisez pas de notes rédigées, sauf éventuellement l'introduction, les transitions et la conclusion.

#### Charter or check? A Carolina furniture maker's family weighs its future

*A private equity fund wants to buy a family-owned furniture maker and grow it fast. Its president would rather write the company's duties to its town into its charter.*

*By Nadia Pemberton-Ruiz (fictitious author)*

In the finishing room of Hollowbrook Furniture Company, Darnell Fairleigh-Moss  
runs his hand along a walnut dresser. His grandfather sanded tables in the same  
building. "Around here, everybody has a cousin at Hollowbrook," he says. The  
fictitious family-owned firm employs about twelve hundred people in two plants in  
L05 a small town in the North Carolina foothills.  
Such loyalty has become rare in American furniture making. According to the Bureau  
of Labor Statistics (BLS), factories making furniture and related products  
employed about 680,000 people in 2000, but only about 338,000 in 2025. Hollowbrook  
has survived decades of cheaper imports by selling solid-wood furniture made to order.  
L10 This fall, a private equity fund offered to buy a majority stake from the  
family. It promises to double the size of the business, partly by offshoring its  
cheaper ranges. For some of the twenty-odd family shareholders, most of whom have  
never worked in the plants, it is a chance to cash in.  
"Our grandparents built this company to provide for their family," says  
L15 Dalton Brannock-Ivey, a cousin who lives in Atlanta. "Shareholders are the owners.  
We have every right to get the best price for what we own." To  
him, shareholder primacy is common sense. A mission written into the charter, he

Claire Brannock, the third-generation president who also chairs the board of  
L20 directors, wants the opposite. She proposes turning Hollowbrook into a public  
benefit corporation, or PBC. Delaware, where the company is incorporated, has  
allowed this status since 2013. A PBC is still a for-profit company, but its  
charter must name one or more public benefits it intends to produce. Its directors  
must balance the shareholders' financial interests, the interests of those  
L25 materially affected by its activity, and that public benefit. At least every two  
years, it must report to its shareholders on its objectives, how it measures  
progress and its results: in effect, an impact report.  
Hollowbrook's mission would have two parts: keeping skilled manufacturing jobs in  
the town and buying wood only from sustainably managed forests. "A promise lasts  
L30 until the next owner," Ms. Brannock says. "A charter is harder to tear up."  
She presents the plan as the triple bottom line in practice. For people, the  
company runs a paid apprenticeship with the local community college and trains  
workers at its own learning center. For the planet, it buys certified hardwood,  
burns its sawdust to heat the drying kilns and has put solar panels on  
L35 one plant's roof. For profit, it charges more than importers and accepts thinner  
margins. "We actually sold more dining tables once we explained where the wood  
came from," she says.  
Supporters see other benefits. A clear mission helps to recruit young people who  
might otherwise leave for the city, and it builds a customer loyalty that  
L40 advertising cannot buy. Mr. Fairleigh-Moss is blunter: "If they sell to a fund,  
I'll eventually be training my replacement overseas."  
The family quarrel echoes a national debate. In August 2019, 181 chief executives

stakeholders, breaking with earlier versions that put shareholders first. Since  
L45 then, an ESG backlash has made stakeholder capitalism a political target. The
