## 2. Synthèse : éléments attendus

*Note pédagogique : la synthèse dure environ 5 minutes ; elle suit la logique de l'auteur, pas l'ordre des paragraphes, et ne contient aucune opinion (JUR-E02, JUR-E04). Toute citation est signalée avec ses lignes (JUR-E03). Les dates du calendrier européen servent l'argument (la règle arrive, puis elle est en partie reportée) : il ne faut pas les réciter toutes.*

**Presenting the document** — This article, entitled "Built in Ohio, checked in Brussels: the new reality of workplace AI", was published in *The Great Lakes Commerce Dispatch*, a (fictitious) American business newspaper, on September 21, 2026. Written by Dana Whitcombe, it uses the case of a fictitious Ohio software company to show how the EU's Artificial Intelligence Act affects American firms that use and sell generative AI.

**Main idea** — The author argues that, despite the postponement of part of the European rules and the opposite direction taken in Washington, American companies that sell in Europe have an interest in preparing now and may find it simpler to apply the European standard everywhere (l. 27-31, 52-55).

**Logical structure of the argument**
1. **Starting point: a concrete case** — A generative AI assistant adopted for efficiency at Corvane Systems now requires a heavy compliance file because a third of its clients are in Europe (l. 1-6).
2. **Context: AI adoption is widespread but uneven** — 17 to 20 percent of US businesses used AI between December 2025 and May 2026, 37 percent of large firms; about 20 percent of EU enterprises in 2025; 32.7 percent of people aged 16 to 74 in the EU used generative AI tools, 15.1 percent for work (l. 7-12).
3. **The European rules and their reach** — The AI Act has been in force since August 2024, with bans and an AI literacy duty (softened in July 2026) since February 2025 and many rules applicable since August 2, 2026, including transparency for chatbots; it applies to non-EU firms whose systems or outputs reach Europe ("the law follows the product, not the passport", l. 19) (l. 13-21).
4. **A partial retreat** — The digital simplification package (proposed November 2025, in force July 2026) postponed high-risk obligations to December 2027 and August 2028 (l. 22-26).
5. **The author's thesis illustrated** — Corvane applies the European rules everywhere because customers ask the same questions; this recalls the "Brussels effect" seen with data protection (l. 27-35).
6. **Objections** — Washington pushes the other way (executive order of December 11, 2025, for a single national framework and against state laws); delays suggest the rules are negotiable; compliance costs may favor Big Tech over startups (l. 36-46).
7. **A blind spot: energy** — Data centers used 4.4 percent of US electricity in 2023, possibly 6.7 to 12 percent by 2028; buyers now ask about power consumption as well as safety (l. 47-51).
8. **Conclusion of the author** — The delay is "breathing room, not an exemption" (l. 52); companies that prepare will cope, those that wait may lose European customers (l. 52-55).

**Transition to the commentary (to be said in English, JUR-E05)** — "Now that I have summarized the article, I will turn to the question: should American companies treat the EU's AI rules as a global standard to adopt rather than a foreign constraint to minimize? I will first explain why adopting them can be a smart business choice, then examine the limits of this strategy, before suggesting a middle way."

## 3. Commentaire : plans possibles

*Note pédagogique : tout plan cohérent qui répond à la question, annonce ses axes et les tient est accepté (JUR-C01, JUR-C07). La valeur ajoutée par rapport à l'auteur (confirmer, nuancer, contredire, illustrer) est décisive (JUR-C06).*

**Plan A — Yes, but… (business case vs limits)**
- Problematic: Is aligning with European AI rules a cost of doing business in Europe or a competitive advantage worldwide?
- I. Reasons to adopt: market access (a single market of some 450 million people), one product instead of two, customer trust (transparency, human oversight), anticipation of other regulations (US states, UK, Asia), precedent of the GDPR (the Irish regulator fined TikTok €530 million in May 2025 over data transfers).
- II. Limits: compliance cost for small firms, risk of slower innovation, uncertainty created by the postponement, a US federal policy that favors deregulation, divergence between US states.
- III. A middle way: adopt the principles (risk mapping, documentation, AI literacy, human review) everywhere, apply the strictest formal obligations only where required; use the delay to prepare.

**Plan B — By stakeholder**
- I. For large US tech groups: the EU is a key market; they can afford compliance and may even benefit (barrier to entry).
- II. For small and mid-size firms like Corvane: higher relative cost, but also a selling point with European clients.
- III. For employees and customers: protection against biased hiring tools, chatbots that must say they are machines; for the planet: energy use of AI (data centers) that the rules barely address.

**Plan C — Intercultural: two philosophies of regulation**
- I. The European approach: precaution, rights, risk-based rules written before harm ("rules first").
- II. The American approach: innovation first, courts and markets as correctives, federal–state tensions (executive order of December 2025).
- III. Convergence or divergence? The Brussels effect worked for data protection; for AI, the postponement and US policy make it less certain; companies, not governments, may set the de facto standard.

**Useful notions from the programme** — `2.1.d` generative AI at work, hallucinations, bias ; `2.4.c` EU regulations (GDPR, DSA, DMA, AI Act) in principle and dated ; `2.3.c` dehumanization of work and impact on the planet ; `2.2.c` efficiency of automated processes (sans technicité excessive, JUR-T05).

## 7. Erreurs fréquentes à éviter (rapports du jury)

- `JUR-T02` — omettre la synthèse : « fortement pénalisés, quelles que soient leurs compétences de communication en anglais ».
- `JUR-E02` — synthèse linéaire qui récite le calendrier européen sans dire à quoi il sert dans l'argument de l'auteur.

- `JUR-C04` — placage de cours sur le fonctionnement technique de l'IA (hors programme : `2.1.d` exclut le fonctionnement des modèles).
- `JUR-T05` — technicité excessive : niveaux de risque détaillés, articles du règlement, montants d'amendes non vérifiés.
- `JUR-E08` — lecture de notes rédigées.
- `JUR-L06` — chiffres et dates mal dits (« thirty-two comma seven », « the 21 September » au lieu du format américain).
- Propre au sujet : présenter le report de l'Omnibus comme une suppression des règles ; confondre la date d'entrée en vigueur (août 2024) et celle d'application générale (août 2026) ; prendre Corvane Systems pour une entreprise réelle.
