## Présentation

Vous disposez d'une heure de préparation. Vous présenterez ensuite, en anglais, un exposé de 15 minutes au maximum en deux phases :

1. la **synthèse** du document ci-dessous : présentez le document, dégagez l'idée directrice et l'articulation logique de l'argumentation de l'auteur, reformulez dans vos propres termes, sans commentaire personnel ; toute citation est signalée avec ses numéros de ligne ; les données chiffrées sont intégrées ;
2. le **commentaire organisé** du document à partir de la question posée : annoncez votre plan, envisagez la question sous plusieurs aspects, appuyez-vous sur vos connaissances, sur l'actualité et, le cas échéant, sur votre expérience professionnelle.

Annoncez clairement, en anglais, le passage de la synthèse au commentaire. L'exposé est suivi d'un **entretien en anglais** de 15 minutes au maximum avec la commission, portant sur le document, votre exposé et d'autres aspects de la question.

Ne lisez pas de notes rédigées, sauf éventuellement l'introduction, les transitions et la conclusion.

#### Sending pounds home: whose money should a Leicester app trust?

*A remittance app weighs a pound-backed stablecoin against the banks as regulators redraw the rules of money.*

*By Imogen Ashworth-Lyle (fictitious author)*

Tellanby, a fictitious e-money firm in Leicester with about a hundred and twenty  
staff, lets thousands of people send money through its app to relatives in India,  
Kenya and Nigeria. Its two founders, both children of immigrants, watched their  
parents queue at transfer counters. Its board, including a venture capital investor,  
L05 must choose: settle transfers with a stablecoin, wait for a public digital pound, or  
stay with the banks.

country. Yet sending money remains costly, a barrier to financial inclusion. The  
L10 United Nations wants the cost of remittances cut below 3 per cent of the sum sent by  
2030, with no route above 5 per cent. In the third quarter of 2025, sending $200  
still cost 6.36 per cent on average worldwide, according to the World Bank, which  
tracks these flows and prices.  
Only Bank of England notes and Royal Mint coins are legal tender in England and  
L15 Wales, yet they make up about 3 per cent of all money, says the Bank. Most of the  
rest is bank deposits. When customers top up the app, their pounds become e-money.  
Unlike deposits, it is not covered by the scheme that compensates savers when a bank  
fails; the firm must keep customers' funds separate, under rules the Financial  
Conduct Authority (FCA) tightened in May 2026.  
L20 Most deposits come from lending: as the Bank explained in 2014, commercial banks  
create new money whenever they grant a loan. The central bank steers this money  
supply through its interest rate. On 5 November 2026, it raised Bank Rate by a  
quarter of a point to 4 per cent. Inflation reached 3.6 per cent in October, and the  
Bank expects about 5 per cent by mid-2027.  
L25 Meera Dabhi-Walsh, Tellanby's co-founder and chief executive, wants a stablecoin: a

L30 In November 2014, the FCA fined five banks a total of £1.1 billion after their

L35 Fiona Castellane, a fictitious former central banker at the fictitious Kenwick

L40 June 2026: at least 30 per cent of the backing held as deposits at the central bank,

L45 and for banks; the Bank says it would not see how people spend, and a digital pound

### Question

**Should the money we use every day be issued by private companies, or remain in the hands of banks and central banks?**
