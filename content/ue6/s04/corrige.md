## 2. Synthèse : éléments attendus

*Note pédagogique : la synthèse dure environ 5 minutes ; elle suit la logique de l'auteur, pas l'ordre des paragraphes, et ne contient aucune opinion (JUR-E02, JUR-E04). Toute citation est signalée avec ses lignes (JUR-E03). Les quatre options de la boulangerie structurent le texte : il faut les regrouper (financement interne, puis trois financements externes) plutôt que les réciter.*

**Presenting the document** — This article, entitled "Neighbors, not bankers? A New Mexico bakery tests the limits of local finance", was published in *The Rio Grande Commerce Courier*, a (fictitious) regional American business newspaper, on October 30, 2026. Written by Lena Marchetti-Hollis, it uses the case of a fictitious family bakery in northern New Mexico to examine how small businesses can finance their growth when interest rates rise and banks become more cautious.

**Main idea** — The author suggests that community-based finance, such as credit unions and local crowdfunding, can partly fill the gap left by big banks and promotes financial inclusion (l. 41-45). However, these tools raise small sums, carry risks for small savers and are themselves hit by higher rates, so they may fund survival rather than growth (l. 46-53, 57-60).

**Logical structure of the argument**
1. **A concrete case** — A successful family bakery needs a bigger oven and a second shop but lacks the money (l. 1-6).
2. **A context of expensive credit** — In September 2026 the Fed raised its key rate by a quarter point to 3.75-4 percent and kept it on hold on October 28 (l. 7-10); small firms already struggled to borrow: in late 2024, 37 percent had applied for credit and only 41 percent of applicants got all they wanted, while 24 percent got nothing (l. 11-14); lenders now look harder at collateral and creditworthiness (l. 15).
3. **Internal funding** — Retained earnings cost no interest and create no debt, but they are slow; the opportunity may disappear (l. 16-19).
4. **External funding 1: the credit union** — A member-owned, not-for-profit cooperative; credit unions are widespread (4,250 federally insured credit unions, 145.8 million members in early 2026) (l. 20-23); they know their borrowers and judge them on their record, not just on a score (l. 23-27); but they can only lend what they collect, and higher rates raise their own costs (l. 27-29).
5. **External funding 2: online lenders** — Fintech lenders are fast but more expensive, and net satisfaction among online-lender applicants fell from 15 to 2 percent between 2023 and 2024, a drop of 13 percentage points (l. 30-34).
6. **External funding 3: crowdfunding** — Federal rules allow small companies to raise up to $5 million a year from ordinary investors through a registered online platform; the bakery's campaign is much smaller: customers lend and are repaid with interest (l. 35-40).
7. **The thesis** — For supporters, credit unions and the crowd fill the gap left by big banks: financial inclusion "close to home" (l. 41-42), in a world where 1.3 billion adults lack access to financial services and where firms with little collateral can be underbanked (l. 42-45).
8. **Objections** — Community finance raises small sums (federal microloans: up to $50,000, about $13,000 on average) (l. 46-50); lending neighbors take risks with their savings: "friendship is not collateral" (l. 50-51); credit unions are not protected from the rate hike (l. 52-53).
9. **A comparison with Europe** — In France and much of the EU, small firms depend even more on banks, so they have fewer alternatives (l. 54-56).
10. **An open conclusion** — The bakery will probably combine sources; whether local money can finance growth, "and not just survival", remains open (l. 57-60).

**Transition to the commentary (to be said in English, JUR-E05)** — "Now that I have summarized the article, I will turn to the question: to what extent can community-based finance fill the gap left by mainstream banks for small businesses? I will first show what local lenders and the crowd do better than big banks, then explain why they cannot replace them, before suggesting how the two can work together."

## 3. Commentaire : plans possibles

*Note pédagogique : tout plan cohérent qui répond à la question, annonce ses axes et les tient est accepté (JUR-C01, JUR-C07). La valeur ajoutée par rapport à l'auteur (confirmer, nuancer, contredire, illustrer) est décisive (JUR-C06). « To what extent » appelle une réponse graduée, pas un oui ou un non.*

**Plan A — A real but partial answer**
- Problematic: Is community-based finance a substitute for bank credit, or only a complement to it?
- I. What community finance does better: local knowledge and trust ("relationship lending" vs credit scores and models); member ownership and no shareholders to pay; inclusion of firms and people that banks overlook (underbanked entrepreneurs, rural areas); money that stays in the community; microcredit as a tool against poverty (Grameen Bank, Nobel Peace Prize 2006).
- II. Its limits: small amounts (microloans, local campaigns) vs the needs of growth; credit unions lend only what they collect and also pay more to savers when rates rise; crowdfunding puts risk on small, often uninformed investors; online lenders are quick but expensive, and borrower satisfaction is falling.
- III. A complement, not a substitute: combining sources (retained earnings, a credit union loan, a crowd campaign, public programs such as SBA microloans); the role of public policy (guarantees, investor protection, EU and US crowdfunding rules); the choice depends on the project (a new oven vs a second shop).

**Plan B — By type of funding (internal vs external)**
- I. Internal funding first: retained earnings keep control and avoid debt, but they are slow and limited, especially when margins are squeezed by rising costs.
- II. External funding from institutions: big banks (cautious, collateral-based), credit unions (closer, cheaper, but smaller), fintech lenders (fast, costly).
- III. External funding from the crowd: rewards, loans or shares; marketing value (customers become ambassadors); risks of failure and conflicts with lenders who are also friends.

**Plan C — Intercultural: the United States, France and Europe, the developing world**
- I. The United States: a dense network of credit unions and community lenders, an active crowdfunding market, but banks and capital markets still dominate business finance.
- II. France and Europe: a bank-based system in which cooperative banks are themselves "mainstream" (Crédit Agricole, Crédit Mutuel, the Banque Populaire and Caisse d'Epargne networks); an EU crowdfunding passport since 2021; small firms report higher loan rates and less available bank credit in 2026.
- III. Developing economies: microcredit and mobile money as tools of financial inclusion; 1.3 billion adults still lack access; limits of microcredit (over-indebtedness, high rates).

**Useful notions from the programme** — `3.3.b` loan, debt, interest burden, default ; `3.3.c` crowdfunding (reward, equity, lending), microfinance, microcredit, financial inclusion ; `3.3.d` retained earnings, self-financing, external finance, cash flow ; `3.1.a` retail bank, deposit, loan, interest rate, credit union (cooperative) ; `3.1.d` fintech, online lender (sans technicité excessive, JUR-T05).

## 7. Erreurs fréquentes à éviter (rapports du jury)

- `JUR-T02` — omettre la synthèse : « fortement pénalisés, quelles que soient leurs compétences de communication en anglais ».
- `JUR-E02` — synthèse linéaire qui décrit les quatre options l'une après l'autre sans dégager la thèse ni les objections.
- `JUR-E04` — glisser une opinion dans la synthèse (« crowdfunding is the best solution »).
- `JUR-C03` — éluder la question : parler de financement participatif en général sans le confronter au rôle des banques.
- `JUR-C04` — placage de cours de finance d'entreprise (structure financière, effet de levier, coût de la dette : hors sujet en UE 6).
- `JUR-T05` — technicité excessive : règles prudentielles, régime juridique détaillé du financement participatif, taux des prêteurs en ligne cités sans source.
- `JUR-E08` — lecture de notes rédigées.
- `JUR-L06` — chiffres et dates mal dits (« one comma three billion » pour 1.3 billion ; « billion » traduit par « billion » en français ; une baisse de 15 % à 2 % décrite comme « a thirteen percent fall »).
- Propre au sujet : confondre *credit union* et banque commerciale ; présenter les prêteurs en ligne comme de la finance « communautaire » ; affirmer que la Fed a relevé ses taux en octobre 2026 (elle les a maintenus) ; prendre Red Mesa Community Credit Union, Horno Azul Bakery ou *The Rio Grande Commerce Courier* pour des entités réelles.
