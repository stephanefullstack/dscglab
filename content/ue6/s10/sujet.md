## Présentation

Vous disposez d'une heure de préparation. Vous présenterez ensuite, en anglais, un exposé de 15 minutes au maximum en deux phases :

1. la **synthèse** du document ci-dessous : présentez le document, dégagez l'idée directrice et l'articulation logique de l'argumentation de l'auteur, reformulez dans vos propres termes, sans commentaire personnel ; toute citation est signalée avec ses numéros de ligne ; les données chiffrées sont intégrées ;
2. le **commentaire organisé** du document à partir de la question posée : annoncez votre plan, envisagez la question sous plusieurs aspects, appuyez-vous sur vos connaissances, sur l'actualité et, le cas échéant, sur votre expérience professionnelle.

Annoncez clairement, en anglais, le passage de la synthèse au commentaire. L'exposé est suivi d'un **entretien en anglais** de 15 minutes au maximum avec la commission, portant sur le document, votre exposé et d'autres aspects de la question.

Ne lisez pas de notes rédigées, sauf éventuellement l'introduction, les transitions et la conclusion.

#### The hits the balance sheet can't hear

*A Nashville music publisher is worth far more on the stock market than in its own accounts.*

*By Corinne Abernathy-Vale (fictitious author)*

On Nashville's Music Row, Lacey Truett-Odom tries a new chorus on a piano.  
"Every hit starts in a room like this," she says. "You won't find this room  
on the balance sheet."  
Larchmoor Songs, the fictitious publisher that signed her, is listed on the  
L05 Nasdaq, employs about three hundred people and manages some sixty thousand  
songs. It is not a record label: it makes no recordings, but owns the songs  
and collects royalties whenever they are streamed, broadcast or used in  
movies. About half of its catalog was bought from songwriters or their heirs;  
its own writers wrote the rest.  
L10 Streaming has given old songs a second life. According to CISAC, the global  
body of authors' societies, royalties collected by its members for music  
creators reached €14.06 billion in 2025, and digital uses such as streaming  
brought in 45.6 percent of that total. Buyers paid high prices for catalogs,  
often with borrowed money, and rising interest rates made those deals  
L15 costlier. Larchmoor borrowed too.  
Today its shares are worth about three times its book value, or shareholders'  
equity. For Halvergate Capital, a fictitious activist investor that built a  
stake this fall, the share price rests on hopes and the company carries too  
much debt. It wants Larchmoor to stop buying catalogs with borrowed money and  
L20 to launch a share buyback.  
Danielle Okafor-Hale, the chief financial officer, sees "hidden value." The  
balance sheet shows the catalogs Larchmoor bought, at the price paid, minus  
amortization, plus goodwill from its acquisitions. It does not show the songs  
written in-house, the Larchmoor name, the ear of its talent scouts or its  
L25 ties with artists. Its tangible assets are modest: an office, two studios,  
some equipment. "Our accounts were built to count machines and stockrooms,  
not choruses," she says. "Our market capitalization tells you more about us  
than our balance sheet."  
Under US GAAP, the American accounting standards, an intangible asset that is  
L30 bought goes on the balance sheet, while most of the cost of building one  
in-house is expensed. In December 2024, the Financial Accounting Standards  
Board asked whether this should change; replies in 2025 showed little support  
for recognizing home-grown intangibles, but some for better disclosure.  
Grant Vesely-Moore, a Halvergate partner, disagrees. "Prudence is not a flaw.  
L35 A balance sheet should protect investors and lenders, not flatter managers.  
What is not on it is only a promise." Market values swing with moods and  
interest rates, he warns; today's price may be a bubble. Goodwill often  
measures the premium paid: "When it is cut through an impairment, that is the  
bill for overpaying." Why ask a company whose shares look expensive to buy  
L40 them back? "Every dollar returned to shareholders is a dollar not spent on an  
overpriced catalog."  
Ms. Truett-Odom has a different complaint. "Everybody argues about what our  
songs are worth. Nobody asks the people who wrote them." She can check the  
share price every minute, but a royalty statement takes her months to  
L45 understand. She wants the annual report to show how much Larchmoor pays its  
writers, and how.  
Since 2005, under a European Union regulation of 2002, listed companies in  
the EU have prepared their consolidated accounts under IFRS. The IFRS  
Foundation profiles 170 jurisdictions; in most of them, companies must use  
L50 its standards. Not in the United States: American listed companies must still  
use US GAAP. Yet a European rival faces the same problem: under both sets of  
rules, home-grown brands never reach the balance sheet, and most home-grown  
songs stay off it too. In July 2026, the World Intellectual Property  
Organization reported that investment in intangible assets in 29 economies  
L55 passed $10 trillion in 2025, after growing more than three times faster than  
tangible investment since 2008.  
For Ruth Adeyemi-Kline, an accounting professor at the equally fictitious  
Caney Fork State University, valuing a song or a brand created in-house is  
subjective, and figures nobody can check are easy to manipulate. Users want  
L60 different things: a lender looks at the cash the catalogs bring in, a  
shareholder at future growth, a songwriter at fair pay. Much information  
already exists outside the balance sheet: the management report, streaming  
figures, how long the rights last. "The answer is not a bigger balance  
sheet," she says, "but a better story around it."  
L65 Larchmoor's board will answer the fund in December. A balance sheet that  
leaves out most of what a company owns tells investors only half the story.  
The other half deserves telling, in figures someone can check.  

### Question

**Is a balance sheet still useful when a company's main assets are songs, brands and people?**
